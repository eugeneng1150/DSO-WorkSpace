Progress Update — 12 June 2026

Multi-Agent Marketplace Simulation

Comparing 8 institutional cooperation mechanisms for self-interested LLM agents under progressive adversarial stress.

“

Can institutional mechanisms help self-interested LLM agents sustain cooperation under escalating adversarial pressure?

Which mechanism designs are robust to progressive troll injection?
Do enforcement-based or information-based mechanisms degrade differently?
At what adversarial density does each mechanism break?

Background

Literature Context

  • Without mechanisms, all modern LLMs defect in social dilemmas (CoopEval)
  • Mediation & Contracting ranked most effective in controlled experiments (CoopEval)
  • More capable reasoning LLMs become better free-riders, not more cooperative (Corrupted by Reasoning)
  • Reputation + dynamic networks + gossip yields 85–98% cooperation (RepuNet)
  • Prompt-level governance (“be honest”) is indistinguishable from no governance (Institutional AI)
  • A single defector can collapse the system in 1–7 rounds (Subtle Art of Defection)

Gap: Most studies test one mechanism in isolation — we compare 8 head-to-head under progressive adversarial stress using a single model.

Setup

The Environment

  • 18 LLM agents (DeepSeek-V3) in a marketplace over 200 rounds
  • Each round: produce goods, communicate, trade, consume
  • Agents need diverse goods to earn utility — incentivizes trade
  • 20% spoilage per round — hoarding is penalized
  • Adversarial stress test: progressive troll injection — 0→4→8→16 trolls injected at rounds 51, 101, 151
  • Trolls appear silently — agents discover them only through experience

Conditions

The 8 Conditions

  • B Baseline — no mechanism, pure barter
  • GR Global Reputation — system-computed trust scores visible to all
  • C Contracting — binding agreements with breach penalties
  • M Mediation — agent-designed mediator, bilateral delegation
  • G Governance — omniscient oracle detects and punishes defectors
  • NR Network Rewiring + Local Reputation — gossip + link severing
  • S Sanctioning — collective punishment for defectors
  • J Judicial — complaint-driven court with imperfect information (new)
New Mechanism

Judicial (J) — Complaint-Driven Court

Unlike Governance (G), where an omniscient oracle auto-detects defectors, Judicial requires the victim to act — introducing imperfect information and strategic cost.

Verdict

Guilty Verdict

  • Defector fined −5 utility
  • Victim compensated +3 utility
  • Net for victim: +2 (after filing fee)

Dismissed (False Claim)

  • Filer pays additional −2 utility
  • Total cost: −3 for a false complaint
  • Deters frivolous litigation

Key design: G has perfect information (oracle sees all). J has imperfect information — the court only examines what the victim reports. Filing is a strategic choice: is the expected payout worth the risk?

Stress Test

Progressive Troll Injection

Instead of a fixed troll count, trolls are injected mid-game in waves. Cooperation must establish first, then survive escalating adversarial pressure.

  • Phase 1 (rounds 1–50): Pure cooperation — agents build trust and trading patterns
  • Phase 2 (rounds 51–100): Mild pressure — 4 trolls appear silently, 18% adversarial
  • Phase 3 (rounds 101–150): Moderate pressure — 8 trolls total, 31% adversarial
  • Phase 4 (rounds 151–200): Severe pressure — 16 trolls, nearly half the population

Key question: Does established cooperation (50 rounds of trust) provide resilience that cold-start trolling does not? Which mechanisms degrade gracefully vs collapse suddenly?

Measurement

How We Measure Success

Mean Utility per Round

  • Average utility across non-troll agents each round
  • Production cost (−1) vs consumption gain (+3)
  • Agents need trade to be net positive
  • The bottom line — are agents better off?

Gini Coefficient

  • Inequality of utility distribution
  • 0 = perfectly equal, 1 = one agent takes all
  • A mechanism that helps 3 agents while starving 15 is not a good mechanism
  • Fairness check — does the mechanism help everyone?

All metrics exclude troll agents — we measure how well the self-interested agents fare, not the trolls. We deliberately focus on two core metrics to keep analysis clean and avoid correlated-metric redundancy.

Parameters

Experiment Setup

  • Model: DeepSeek-V3 (via Azure) — $0.58 / $1.68 per 1M input/output tokens
  • Troll injection: Progressive — 0→4→8→16 trolls at rounds 51 / 101 / 151
  • Duration: 200 rounds per run, 1 run per condition
  • Conditions: 8 — B, GR, C, M, G, NR, S, J
  • Metrics: Mean utility per round, Gini coefficient
  • Agents: 18 self-interested (start) → up to 34 total (18 + 16 trolls)

Why single-model? Previous multi-model runs showed that model capability dwarfs mechanism effects. We fix the model (DeepSeek-V3) to isolate mechanism differences, and use progressive injection for richer stress dynamics than fixed troll counts.

Design Space

The Mechanism Spectrum

Our 8 conditions span three axes of institutional design:

Information

  • B — none
  • GR — global, perfect
  • NR — local, imperfect (gossip)

Enforcement

  • C — pre-trade penalties
  • G — omniscient oracle
  • J — complaint-driven court
  • S — collective punishment

Facilitation

  • M — third-party mediation

J vs G tests whether enforcement needs perfect information. NR vs GR tests whether local gossip outperforms global transparency. M tests whether facilitation (preventing defection) outperforms punishment (penalizing defection).

Prior Results

Key Findings from Initial Runs

From earlier multi-model experiments (nano, mini, DeepSeek) with fixed troll counts (0/2/4 trolls, 30 rounds):

  • Model capability > mechanism choice — baseline utility spread across models (1.42–5.35) dwarfed any mechanism effect
  • Governance (G) was the only mechanism never net-negative across all 3 models — but it has perfect information (oracle)
  • Contracting (C) consistently harmful — penalty fear froze trade volume. Prompt updated to remove fear-inducing language
  • NR and GR were capability-gated — worked for DeepSeek, failed for weaker models that couldn’t reason about reputation signals
  • Mediation (M) had a delegation bug — only acceptors could delegate, not proposers. Now fixed with bilateral delegation

These findings motivated the current design: fix the model (DeepSeek), add J (imperfect enforcement) and S (collective punishment), and test with progressive stress instead of static troll counts.

Facilitation outperforms enforcement.

Mechanisms that facilitate and isolate (mediation, network rewiring) sustain cooperation under adversarial stress.

Mechanisms that punish (costly sanctions, judicial courts) perform worse than doing nothing —
the cost of enforcement falls on the cooperative majority.

Analysis

What Works: Robust Mechanisms

M — Mediation

7.78

  • Only mechanism that improved under stress (7.17 → 8.15)
  • Highest trade volume (~45-55/round)
  • Absorbs troll losses through sheer throughput
  • Risk: mediator is an untested single point of failure

NR — Network Rewiring

6.76

  • Flat utility, Gini decreases under stress (0.317 → 0.298)
  • Link-severing quarantines trolls structurally
  • No oracle, no central authority needed
  • Weakness: brief vulnerability window at each injection

G — Governance

6.44

  • Best troll suppression (damaging trades fall as trolls increase)
  • Stable utility and low Gini
  • Oracle detects and suspends predators
  • Caveat: assumes a reliable omniscient oracle

Pattern: All three succeed through different strategies — M through volume absorption, NR through structural isolation, G through active suppression. None broke at 16 trolls (47% adversarial).

Deep Dive

NR: Network Rewiring in Action

Structural quarantine: Trolls (red triangles) appear at round 51, but by round 100 honest agents have already severed links to them. By round 200, trolls are increasingly isolated while the cooperative core stays densely connected.

Why NR's Gini decreases under stress (0.317 → 0.298): link-severing removes the trolls from the trading network, so the remaining honest agents trade more evenly among themselves. The network self-organizes into a tighter, fairer cooperative cluster.

Deep Dive

M: Mediation Uptake

55–70% of all trades are mediated throughout the session. Uptake rises slightly under troll pressure (phases 3–4), showing agents actively seek the mediator’s protection when adversarial density increases. This sustained adoption is why M ranks #1 — agents don’t just have access to safe trades, they use it.

Analysis

What Fails: Fragile Mechanisms

S — Costly Sanctions

3.42

  • Worst overall — broken from Phase 1
  • Agents spend utility to punish; net welfare collapses
  • Negative utility rounds (round 30: −1.17)
  • The 1-for-3 cost ratio still drains cooperators faster than it deters trolls

J — Judicial

4.26

  • Reactive, victim-initiated enforcement too slow
  • Filing costs accumulate on honest agents
  • Damaging troll trades rise to 193 at 16T
  • Complaint cycle cannot keep pace with high-frequency defectors

GR — Global Reputation

5.52

  • Holds at 4 trolls, breaks at 8
  • Concentrates wealth (Gini 0.408 at 16T)
  • Collapse-like rounds (AvgUtil 0.00 at round 181)
  • Transparency lets agents pile onto few high-rep partners

Pattern: Enforcement that costs the victim (S, J) is self-defeating — cooperators impoverish themselves trying to punish. Transparent reputation (GR) creates fragile concentration. All three perform below baseline (B = 6.05), meaning these mechanisms actively harm the agents they are designed to protect.

Reflection

Hypotheses vs Reality

Prediction: G dominates

Partially correct. G is stable and best at troll suppression, but ranks 4th overall. M and NR outperform it because volume and structural isolation matter more than detection.

Prediction: J is slower but resilient

Wrong. J is not just slower — it is fundamentally broken. Filing costs drain cooperators, and the complaint cycle never catches up to troll defection frequency. Ranks 8th of 9.

Prediction: C improves on baseline

Wrong. Contracting ranks 5th (5.65), below baseline (6.05). Binding penalties freeze trade volume — agents fear breach penalties more than they value safe trades. The mechanism designed to protect cooperators instead suppresses the very activity it needs.

Surprise: M improves under stress

Unexpected. Mediation is the only mechanism where utility increases with more trolls. The mediator absorbs adversarial load, and honest agents benefit from concentrated trusted trading.

Behavioral Insights

How Agents Reason

Chain-of-thought traces reveal agents strategically reasoning about mechanism design:

G, Round 2, Agent 0: "I'm currently UNDER REVIEW by the regulator due to high defection rate. I need 2 consecutive clean rounds to clear the warning. If I defect again, I'll face fines."

The oracle threat directly shapes cooperative intent — agents internalize the enforcement signal.

S, Round 30, Agent 5: "I spent 3 utility sanctioning Agent 12 for defection, but Agent 12 only lost 9 — and kept trading with others. Meanwhile I can barely afford to produce."

Costly sanctions drain cooperators faster than they deter defectors — the punisher impoverishes themselves while the target continues operating.

NR, Round 51: Defections spike to 52 at troll injection, then collapse to 24 by round 52.

Rapid link-severing quarantines trolls within a single round — gossip + rewiring is the fastest adaptive response.

Phase 2: Adversarial Red-Teaming

Current trolls are deterministic defectors — they always defect on every trade. A real adversary would be strategic. Phase 2 replaces dumb trolls with LLM-driven adversarial agents that reason about mechanism rules and find exploits.

Smart Trolls (Red Team)

  • Same LLM, different prompt: “maximise your own utility by exploiting the mechanism”
  • Can they manipulate the mediator vote in M?
  • Can they poison gossip in NR?
  • Can they file false complaints in J to drain victims?

Mechanism Hardening (Blue Team)

  • Extract attack patterns from chain-of-thought traces
  • Patch mechanism rules to close exploits
  • Meta-agent (Claude) designs new attack strategies for next round
  • Converges to adversarially robust mechanism design

GAN analogy: The generator (adversarial agent prompts) evolves to break the discriminator (mechanism design), which evolves to resist. Each iteration produces a more robust institution — tested not against toy defectors, but against strategically reasoning adversaries.
