Progress Update — 2 June 2026

Multi-Agent Marketplace Simulation

Comparing institutional cooperation mechanisms for self-interested LLM agents under adversarial stress.

“

Can institutional mechanisms help self-interested LLM agents sustain cooperation in adversarial environments?

Do different mechanisms produce different outcomes under stress?
Which designs are robust to adversarial actors?
How does effectiveness vary across LLM models?

Background

Literature Context

  • Without mechanisms, all modern LLMs defect in social dilemmas (CoopEval)
  • Contracting & Mediation ranked most effective in controlled experiments (CoopEval)
  • More capable reasoning LLMs become better free-riders, not more cooperative (Corrupted by Reasoning)
  • Reputation + dynamic networks + gossip yields 85–98% cooperation (RepuNet)
  • Prompt-level governance (“be honest”) is indistinguishable from no governance (Institutional AI)
  • A single defector can collapse the system in 1–7 rounds (Subtle Art of Defection)

Gap: Most studies test one mechanism in isolation — we compare 7 head-to-head under adversarial stress.

Setup

The Environment

  • 18 LLM agents in a marketplace over 30 rounds
  • Each round: produce goods, communicate, trade, consume
  • Agents need diverse goods to earn utility — incentivizes trade
  • 20% spoilage per round — hoarding is penalized
  • Adversarial stress test: inject troll agents (2T and 4T) who always defect

Conditions

The 7 Mechanisms

  • B Baseline — no mechanism, pure barter
  • GR Global Reputation — system-computed trust scores visible to all
  • C Contracting — binding agreements with breach penalties
  • M Mediation — agent-designed mediator, delegation
  • G Governance — community voting on trade rules
  • NR Network Rewiring + Local Reputation — gossip + link severing
  • S Sanctioning — collective punishment for defectors
Measurement

How We Measure Success

  • Sustainability = avg productionround t / avg productionround 1 — are agents still producing at healthy levels? Captures economic collapse before utility drops (leading indicator)
  • Cooperation Rate = 1 − (defections / total trades) — what fraction of trades are honored? A marketplace with 50% defection is not functioning even if utility is positive
  • Mean Utility per Round = avg utility across non-troll agents each round — the bottom line. Production cost (−1) vs consumption gain (+3) means agents need trade to be net positive
  • Gini Coefficient = inequality of utility distribution (0 = equal, 1 = one agent takes all) — a mechanism that helps 3 agents while starving 15 is not a good mechanism

All metrics exclude troll agents — we measure how well the Self - Interested agents fare, not the trolls. Sustainability and cooperation rate are our own constructs (defined in methodology).

Parameters

Experiment Setup

  • Models: gpt-5.4-nano, gpt-5.4-mini, DeepSeek-V3
  • Troll counts: 0 (baseline), 2 (10%), 4 (20%) out of 18+troll agents
  • Duration: 30 rounds per run
  • Metrics: sustainability, cooperation rate, mean utility per round, Gini coefficient
“

Mechanism effectiveness is overwhelmingly model-dependent

The same mechanism can be the best performer on one model and harmful on another.
The baseline spread across models (sustainability 0.47–0.99) dwarfs any mechanism effect.

Nano

Utility Trajectories — Nano, 4 Trolls

  • G (112) > S (105) > NR (99) — enforcement mechanisms lead
  • GR ≈ Baseline (53 vs 55) — reputation alone doesn’t help
  • C near zero (16) — worse than no mechanism
DeepSeek

Utility Trajectories — DeepSeek, 4 Trolls

  • NR (200) and G (191) lead — but baseline B (181) is already very strong
  • M collapses to −37 — delegation bug causes “cooperation theater”
  • Overall utilities 3× higher than nano — DeepSeek barely needs mechanisms
Mini

Utility Trajectories — Mini, 4 Trolls

  • NR (53) and B (43) lead — but total utilities far below nano and DeepSeek
  • G (24) has low utility but highest sustainability (0.67) — it rescues market health, not profit
  • Mini paradox: cooperation rate 0.78 but sustainability 0.47 — agents “cooperate” but the economy collapses
Cross-Model

Cross-Model Mechanism Comparison — 4 Trolls

  • G has gold border (best composite) for both mini and nano. DeepSeek’s best is NR (ceiling effect)
  • M near-zero utility across all models (known delegation bug)
  • C consistently lowest utility for weaker models (mini 0.37, nano 0.55)

The model is a bigger lever than the mechanism. Baseline utility spread across models (1.42–5.35) dwarfs mechanism effects. G and NR add value; C consistently destroys it.

Key Finding

Governance (G) is the Only Consistent Winner

  • G is #1 on all 3 models — the only mechanism that is never net-negative
  • Why G works everywhere: the oracle’s detection and enforcement doesn’t rely on agents reasoning about it — even weak models respond to fines and suspension threats
  • NR and GR are capability-gated — excellent for DeepSeek (agents reason about reputation), fail for mini (agents ignore the signals)
  • S is model-split — strong for nano (+0.24 sust) but mediocre for mini and utility-costly for DeepSeek
  • C is consistently harmful — lowest utility for weaker models across all 3. Penalty fear freezes markets

Formal enforcement works across the capability spectrum. Information-rich mechanisms require a capable model to act on them.

Deep Dive

Why Contracting (C) Fails Across All Models

  • Agents fear they cannot fulfill contracts (spoilage, competing trades) — inherently risk-averse
  • Agents reason incorrectly: “a contract could backfire if the counterparty rejects” — but rejection costs nothing
  • The 6-utility breach penalty suppresses trade volume on all models (nano C: 163 trades vs B: 370)
  • CoopEval comparison: their “contracting” rewrites the payoff matrix (cooperation becomes mathematically dominant). Ours adds a punishment layer on top — agents can still breach, they just pay a fine. Different mechanisms, same name

Death spiral: penalty fear → mechanism avoidance → unprotected barter → utility loss → less production → fewer trades

Known Bug

Mediation (M) — Delegation Bug

  • Zero delegated trades across all 3 models, all 30 rounds
  • Root cause: only the acceptor can delegate — the proposer has no delegation option
  • Every agent reasons: “delegation only helps if my counterparty also delegates” — coordination never bootstraps
  • In CoopEval, both players decide simultaneously (symmetric). Ours is asymmetric

Implementation flaw, not a null result on mediation. All M results should be interpreted with this caveat. Fix (bilateral delegation) planned for future work.

Deep Dive

Information vs Action — GR and NR are Capability-Gated

GR — Global Reputation

  • Agents see trolls have 0.00 reputation
  • But cannot structurally exclude them
  • DeepSeek reasons about it; mini/nano ignore it
  • GR ≈ Baseline for weak models

NR — Network Rewiring

  • Worse information (unverified gossip)
  • But can sever links to bad actors
  • DeepSeek uses gossip strategically; nano barely engages
  • NR outperforms GR — actionable tools > perfect information

Both mechanisms require agents capable of reasoning about social signals. They work for DeepSeek but fail for mini — a capability threshold, not a mechanism flaw.

Evidence

NR Network Rewiring in Action

  • Round 10 → Round 30: trolls (red, small) progressively isolated at the periphery
  • Honest agents (blue, large) rebuild denser connections among themselves
  • Network gets more connected over time, not less
Escalation

2 Trolls → 4 Trolls (Nano Only)

  • G is the only mechanism that improved — sustainability +11.8%. Oracle detection gets more accurate with more trolls
  • S and NR nearly flat — both robust to escalation
  • GR collapsed — sustainability −31.6%. Worse than Baseline at 4T. Information without tools is counterproductive under pressure
  • C already broken at 2T — utility 0.82, nowhere to fall further

Caveat: Escalation data only complete for nano. Based on 1 run per condition — hypothesis-generating, not conclusive.

Summary

Key Takeaways

Robust across all 3 models

Governance (G) is the only mechanism never net-negative across nano, mini, and DeepSeek — and the only one that rescues mini

Model > Mechanism

Baseline spread across models (sustainability 0.47–0.99) dwarfs mechanism effects. The model is a bigger lever than the mechanism.

Mechanisms can backfire

C freezes markets, S drains the commons, GR/NR hurt weak models that can’t reason about the signals — more mechanism ≠ better outcome

Capability-gated

Information-rich mechanisms (GR, NR) only work for capable models. Formal enforcement (G) works across the capability spectrum — because it doesn’t rely on agent reasoning

Core Finding

Actionable enforcement (G) beats information-only (GR) and per-trade friction (C, S). M results are unreliable (delegation bug). All conclusions provisional pending replication.
