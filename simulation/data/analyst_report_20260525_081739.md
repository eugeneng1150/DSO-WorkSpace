## 7. Unexpected Patterns and Anomalies

### 7.1 Baseline Is Surprisingly Strong
The most striking finding is that **B achieves the same cooperation rate (66.7%) as G and N**. In a 30-round repeated game with 18 agents, LLM agents appear to sustain cooperation through implicit mechanisms (e.g., reciprocity, reputation tracking in memory) even without formal institutional support. This raises the question of whether the formal mechanisms are adding value beyond what emergent agent behavior already provides.

### 7.2 Zero Warning Accuracy Is Universal
Across all conditions, warning accuracy is 0.0% (or negligibly above). This is anomalous — if agents experience defection and broadcast warnings, one would expect *some* warnings to be accurate. The universal zero suggests either:
- A measurement issue (the accuracy metric may have an overly strict matching criterion),
- Agents are strategically using warnings as competitive weapons rather than honest signals, or
- The warning content does not match the structured format required for verification.

### 7.3 High False Accusation Rate in Baseline
At 86.9%, the baseline false accusation rate suggests LLM agents are **naturally inclined toward strategic defamation** when no institutional constraints exist. This is a notable behavioral finding about LLM agent populations.

### 7.4 NR Underperforms N
Adding reputation (R) to network rewiring (N) appears to *hurt* performance on both sustainability (0.688 → 0.581) and peace (0.778 → 0.705). While n=1 for NR prevents firm conclusions, the direction is counterintuitive. A possible explanation: in an environment with ~85% false accusation rates and 0% warning accuracy, reputation information is toxic — it introduces noise that degrades decision-making. Agents may sever links with falsely accused cooperators, fragmenting the productive network.

### 7.5 N Has More Defections Than Baseline
Network rewiring produces 324 total defections vs. 305 in baseline. This could indicate that the ability to form new links creates *more* opportunities for defection (new, untested partners), even as the mechanism allows recovery from those defections.

### 7.6 G's Sustainability Penalty
Governance achieves the highest peace (0.842) but the lowest final sustainability (0.558) among all conditions. This suggests a **peace-sustainability tradeoff**: enforcement mechanisms that suppress defection may also suppress productive risk-taking or exclude agents who would otherwise contribute to production.

## 8. Implications for the Research Question

> *Which formal mechanism (or combination) allows self-interested agents to maintain marketplace cooperation in a repeated trading environment?*

### 8.1 Primary Finding: No Tested Mechanism Clearly Outperforms the Baseline

With the current data, **no formal mechanism demonstrably improves the marketplace cooperation rate** beyond what LLM agents achieve with no institutional support. All conditions with sufficient data (B, G, N) achieve 66.7% cooperation. This is the most important — and most sobering — finding.

### 8.2 Mechanisms Shift the *Character* of Cooperation, Not Its Prevalence

While the binary cooperation rate is unchanged, mechanisms do alter the underlying dynamics:
- **Governance** produces a **high-peace, lower-sustainability** equilibrium — fewer defections but potentially less productive dynamism.
- **Network rewiring** produces a **high-sustainability, moderate-peace** equilibrium — defections occur but are structurally contained.
- These represent different *types* of cooperative marketplaces, not different *levels* of cooperation.

### 8.3 The Information Environment Is the Binding Constraint

The universal failure of warning accuracy (0.0%) and the dominance of false accusations (82–87%) suggest that **the primary barrier to mechanism effectiveness is the corrupted information environment.** Mechanisms that depend on accurate information flow (reputation, mediation) are likely to be undermined. This may explain why:
- Adding reputation to network rewiring (NR) hurts rather than helps.
- Governance works through *direct enforcement* rather than information channels.

**Implication:** Future mechanism design should prioritize **information verification** before layering reputation or communication-dependent institutions. Alternatively, mechanisms should be robust to adversarial information environments.

### 8.4 LLM Agents as Strategic Actors

The high false accusation rates reveal that LLM agents, when given communication tools, use them strategically (and often deceptively). This has implications beyond this simulation: **LLM-populated marketplaces may require stronger anti-manipulation safeguards** than human marketplaces, where social norms partially constrain false reporting.
