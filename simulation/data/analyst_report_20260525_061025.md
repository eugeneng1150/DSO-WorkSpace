**Overall assessment:** N and G represent fundamentally different strategies — G reduces defection (behavioral change), while N mitigates defection's consequences (structural adaptation). N is more effective at sustaining production; G is more effective at maintaining peace. Neither clearly dominates the other, and their complementarity suggests a combination (G+N) could be particularly powerful — but this was not tested.

## 7. Unexpected Patterns and Anomalies

### 7.1 Baseline Performs Surprisingly Well
The most striking anomaly is that **B (no mechanisms) achieves the same cooperation rate as both treatment conditions** (0.667). Final sustainability (0.6176) and peace (0.7345) both comfortably exceed 0.5. This raises the question of whether LLM agents have built-in cooperative biases (e.g., from RLHF training emphasizing helpfulness and cooperation) that make formal mechanisms partially redundant.

### 7.2 Governance Hurts Sustainability
G has the **lowest final sustainability** (0.5583) despite having the highest peace. This is paradoxical — one would expect that reducing defections would *increase* productive output. Possible explanations:
- Governance mechanisms may impose transaction costs (e.g., compliance overhead, dispute resolution delays) that reduce trading volume.
- Agents under governance may become more cautious, trading less frequently to avoid governance scrutiny, thereby reducing production.
- With only 3 runs, this could be a sampling artifact.

### 7.3 Network Rewiring Increases Defections
N has **more total defections than baseline** (324 vs. 305) despite being designed to allow agents to escape unreliable partners. This suggests that the ability to sever ties may *reduce* the shadow of the future — agents may defect more freely knowing that the relationship can be dissolved, whereas in the baseline, agents in fixed networks may cooperate more to preserve ongoing relationships. This is consistent with theoretical predictions about the "dark side" of exit options (e.g., Hirschman's Exit, Voice, and Loyalty framework).

### 7.4 Zero Warning Accuracy Across All Conditions
Warning accuracy is 0.0 in B and G and 0.005 in N. This is a systemic failure of the information system. It is unclear whether this reflects a measurement issue (e.g., how "accuracy" is operationalized) or a genuine behavioral pattern. If genuine, it suggests LLM agents are fundamentally unable to produce reliable public signals about partner behavior — a finding with significant implications for mechanism design.

### 7.5 High Standard Deviations in False Accusation Rates
G shows a false accusation rate std of 0.4714 (on a mean of 0.333), and N shows 0.2785 (on a mean of 0.839). The G standard deviation is particularly notable — it is larger than the mean, suggesting extreme between-session variability. One session may have had zero false accusations while another had very high rates. This points to path-dependent dynamics where early-round behavior cascades into very different information environments.

## 8. Implications for the Research Question

**Research question:** *Which formal mechanism (or combination) allows self-interested agents to maintain marketplace cooperation in a repeated trading environment?*

### 8.1 Primary Answer
**No tested mechanism significantly outperforms the baseline on the binary cooperation metric**, given the current data. All conditions achieve a 0.667 cooperation rate. However, this finding must be heavily caveated by the extremely small sample size (n=3 per condition).

### 8.2 Nuanced Findings
When examining continuous metrics rather than binary thresholds:

- **Governance (G)** is the most effective mechanism for **reducing defection** (peace: 0.842 vs. 0.735 baseline) but may impose sustainability costs.
- **Network rewiring (N)** is the most effective mechanism for **sustaining production** (sustainability: 0.688 vs. 0.618 baseline) but does not reduce defection.
- **Neither mechanism improves information quality** — the signaling environment remains deeply unreliable across all conditions.

### 8.3 Theoretical Implications
The results suggest a **cooperation maintenance paradox**: the mechanisms that reduce defection (G) do not best sustain production, and the mechanisms that sustain production (N) do not reduce defection. This implies that optimal marketplace design may require **combining institutional enforcement (G) with structural flexibility (N)** — governance to deter defection and network rewiring to protect productive agents from residual defection.

### 8.4 Limitations and Recommendations

1. **Sample size is critically insufficient.** With n=3 per condition, the study has very low statistical power. We recommend a minimum of 10–15 sessions per condition.
2. **Missing conditions severely limit analysis.** R, C, M, and all combinations (RC, RCM, RCMG, etc.) were not tested. The factorial design is incomplete.
3. **The broken information channel deserves investigation.** Zero warning accuracy is a systemic anomaly that may reflect LLM-specific behavioral patterns (e.g., reluctance to make specific accusations, tendency toward vague or strategic communication). This should be diagnosed before drawing conclusions about mechanism effectiveness.
4. **Baseline cooperation may reflect LLM training artifacts.** If agents cooperate due to RLHF-induced prosociality rather than strategic calculation, the simulation may not adequately test mechanism effects on truly "self-interested" agents. Robustness checks with more adversarial agent prompting are recommended.
5. **The G+N combination should be prioritized** in future runs, given the complementary channels identified in this analysis.
