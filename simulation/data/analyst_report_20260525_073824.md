## 7. Unexpected Patterns and Anomalies

### 7.1 The NR Degradation Effect (Most Anomalous Finding)
Adding reputation (R) to network rewiring (N) **dramatically worsened** every metric. NR had 68% more defections than N alone and 79% more than baseline. This is counterintuitive — reputation should help agents make better rewiring decisions.

**Possible explanations:**
- **Information overload / misuse:** Reputation signals in this environment are unreliable (warning accuracy ≈ 0, false accusation rate ≈ 82%). When agents use reputation to guide rewiring decisions, they may sever links with falsely accused cooperators and maintain links with undetected defectors, producing worse outcomes than random or experience-based rewiring.
- **Strategic manipulation:** Defectors may exploit the reputation system to damage cooperators' standing, making network rewiring counterproductive.
- **Mechanism interference:** The cognitive/computational burden of processing both reputation information and network rewiring decisions may degrade agent decision quality.

### 7.2 Baseline Performs Surprisingly Well
B achieves 66.7% cooperation, matching G and N. This suggests LLM agents have strong intrinsic cooperative tendencies in repeated interactions, even without formal mechanisms. The 30-round horizon with identifiable partners may create sufficient shadow-of-the-future incentives.

### 7.3 Near-Zero Warning Accuracy Across All Conditions
Warning accuracy is 0.0 in B and G and barely above zero elsewhere. This is striking — it means agents are systematically failing to issue accurate warnings even when they experience defection. This could reflect:
- LLM agents' difficulty in accurately categorizing and reporting trade outcomes
- Strategic incentives to issue false warnings against competitors
- A measurement artifact (the accuracy metric may be overly strict)

### 7.4 G Reduces Sustainability Despite Reducing Defection
G has the lowest final sustainability (0.5583) despite having the fewest defections (194). This suggests governance imposes **compliance costs** that reduce productive output — agents may trade less frequently, trade more conservatively, or face governance-related penalties that reduce overall production even as they reduce defection.

### 7.5 High Variance in Sustainability
Sustainability standard deviations are substantial (0.10–0.13) relative to means, suggesting high between-session variability. This is consistent with the small sample size (n=3) and suggests that session-specific dynamics (e.g., early defection cascades, agent personality draws) dominate mechanism effects.

## 8. Implications for the Research Question

**Research question:** *Which formal mechanism (or combination) allows self-interested agents to maintain marketplace cooperation in a repeated trading environment?*

### 8.1 Primary Answer
**No tested mechanism reliably ensures marketplace cooperation.** The best conditions (B, G, N) achieve cooperation in only 2/3 sessions, and this rate is not distinguishable from the mechanism-free baseline. The only combination tested (NR) actively harmed cooperation.

### 8.2 Nuanced Findings

1. **Governance (G) is the most effective single mechanism for reducing defection** but comes at a sustainability cost. It achieves the highest peace (0.842) with the fewest defections (194) but the lowest sustainability (0.558). This suggests a **peace-productivity tradeoff** in institutional design.

2. **Network rewiring (N) is the most effective mechanism for sustaining production** and achieves this through structural adaptation rather than behavioral change. It produces the highest final sustainability (0.688) and modestly improves peace, even without reducing total defections.

3. **Mechanism combinations can be harmful.** The NR condition demonstrates that naively combining mechanisms can produce negative interaction effects, particularly when one mechanism (reputation) feeds unreliable information into another mechanism's decision process (network rewiring). This is a cautionary finding for institutional design.

Any mechanism that relies on accurate peer-to-peer information transmission is undermined by this noise floor. Future designs should prioritize **information quality** before layering mechanisms that depend on it.
