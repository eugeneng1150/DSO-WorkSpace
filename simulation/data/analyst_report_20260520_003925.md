## 6. Unexpected Patterns and Anomalies

### 6.1 Formal Mechanisms Increase Defections
The most striking anomaly: **Contracting and Mediation dramatically increase defections** (C: 1,838; M: 2,065 vs. B: 715). This is the opposite of their intended effect. Possible explanations:
- **Moral hazard**: Agents exploit the formal safety net, defecting more because they expect contracts to be enforced or disputes to be mediated.
- **Strategic manipulation**: Agents may use contracts or mediation processes as tools for exploitation rather than cooperation.
- **Reduced informal enforcement**: Formal mechanisms may crowd out the informal social sanctions (reputation gossip, partner avoidance) that partially constrain defection in the baseline.

### 6.2 Baseline Has the Best Final Peace
The condition with *no* formal mechanisms achieves the highest final-round Peace (0.3416). This suggests that informal, emergent social norms—however weak—are more effective at sustaining cooperation than the formal mechanisms tested.

### 6.3 Mediation Is Consistently the Worst Mechanism
Mediation produces the lowest final Peace (0.1281), the most defections (2,065), and the lowest final Sustainability among single mechanisms (0.5541). It adds negative value in every combination (RM < R on Peace; CM < C on Peace; RCM < RC on Peace). This mechanism appears actively harmful.

### 6.4 RCM Performs Worse Than R Alone on Peace
The full mechanism suite (RCM: final Peace = 0.1618) performs substantially worse than Reputation alone (R: 0.2615) on the critical binding metric. More mechanisms ≠ better outcomes. This suggests mechanism interference or complexity costs.

### 6.5 Sustainability-Peace Divergence
Reputation-containing conditions show a puzzling pattern: high Sustainability but collapsing Peace. Agents maintain production even as defection rates soar. This may indicate that Reputation helps agents form stable sub-networks of cooperators who sustain production, while the broader marketplace descends into defection. The aggregate Peace metric captures the full marketplace, while Sustainability may be sustained by a productive minority.

### 6.6 Crowding Out of Whistleblowing
The near-complete suppression of whistleblowing under formal mechanisms (especially RM = 0.0) is notable. Formal institutions appear to substitute for, rather than complement, informal information sharing—but the formal mechanisms themselves fail to prevent defection, leaving agents worse off than under informal norms alone.

### 6.7 High Variance in Baseline
Baseline Peace has the highest standard deviation (0.1808), suggesting that without formal mechanisms, outcomes are highly path-dependent. Some baseline runs may have achieved near-cooperative outcomes while others collapsed early. This variance is worth investigating at the session level.

## 7. Implications for the Research Question

**Research Question**: *Which formal mechanism (or combination) allows self-interested agents to maintain marketplace cooperation in a repeated trading environment?*

### Primary Answer
**None of the tested mechanisms or combinations achieves marketplace cooperation.** The 0% cooperation rate across all 40 runs (8 conditions × 5 sessions) is a definitive negative result.

### Nuanced Interpretation

1. **The Peace problem is unsolved.** All conditions exhibit late-round Peace collapse, consistent with end-game unraveling in finitely repeated games. Self-interested LLM agents appear to recognize the approaching horizon and increase defection. No tested mechanism provides sufficient deterrence against this dynamic.

2. **Reputation is the only mechanism with partial positive effects**, improving Sustainability substantially (+19.5 pp) and maintaining moderate mean Peace (0.5349). It likely works by enabling partner selection and relationship persistence rather than by deterring defection directly.

3. **Contracting and Mediation are counterproductive.** They increase defections, suppress informal enforcement, and degrade Peace. This challenges the institutional economics assumption that formal enforcement mechanisms straightforwardly improve cooperation. In this LLM agent context, these mechanisms appear to create moral hazard and crowd out emergent social norms.

4. **Mechanism combinations show no positive synergy.** The full RCM suite performs worse than R alone on the binding metric (Peace). Institutional complexity may impose cognitive or strategic costs on agents.

5. **Informal norms partially outperform formal institutions.** The baseline's superior final Peace (0.3416) suggests that the implicit social dynamics among LLM agents—possibly including reciprocity heuristics, partner memory, and natural language negotiation—provide a baseline of cooperation that formal mechanisms disrupt rather than enhance.

### Limitations and Caveats

- **Sample size**: 5 runs per condition is small; high-variance metrics (e.g., baseline Peace σ = 0.18) may not be reliably estimated. Statistical significance testing is not possible with confidence at this sample size.
- **Finite horizon**: The 30-round design with known endpoints creates strong end-game incentives to defect. Mechanisms might perform differently in indefinitely repeated games.
- **LLM-specific behavior**: Results may reflect LLM agents' particular response to mechanism descriptions (e.g., interpreting "contract" as license to defect strategically) rather than general properties of the mechanisms.
- **Mechanism implementation details**: The report cannot assess whether the mechanisms were implemented in ways that faithfully capture their real-world analogs. Poor implementation could explain the negative results.
- **Missing session-level data**: Aggregate statistics may mask heterogeneity. Some individual sessions may have achieved cooperation; the summary statistics do not allow this assessment beyond the cooperative_runs count.

### Recommendations for Future Research

1. **Investigate end-game effects**: Run simulations with uncertain endpoints or infinite-horizon discounting to test whether mechanisms perform better without end-game unraveling.
2. **Redesign Contracting and Mediation**: Current implementations appear to create moral hazard. Consider mechanisms with stronger penalties, automatic enforcement, or reputation-linked consequences for contract violation.
3. **Increase sample size**: 5 runs is insufficient for reliable inference, particularly given observed variance.
4. **Examine session-level trajectories**: Decompose when Peace collapses (early vs. late rounds) and whether any sessions approached cooperation.
5. **Test Reputation enhancements**: Since Reputation is the only partially effective mechanism, test variants (e.g., public reputation scores, reputation decay, reputation-weighted matching) that might strengthen its Peace effects.
6. **Investigate the crowding-out effect**: The suppression of whistleblowing under formal mechanisms deserves dedicated study as a potential general phenomenon in hybrid institutional environments.
