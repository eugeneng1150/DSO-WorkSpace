**Mediation (M):** A paradoxical profile. It produces the **highest mean sustainability** of any single mechanism (0.7524) and strong final sustainability (0.6714), but the **lowest mean peace** (0.3124) and the **highest total defections** (295, more than double baseline). Mediation appears to keep the marketplace economically productive despite rampant defection—possibly because mediation resolves disputes and redistributes resources after defections occur, sustaining production even as trust collapses.

### Verdict:
**No single mechanism is sufficient.** Each addresses at most one dimension. Mediation best sustains production; Reputation marginally improves sustainability; Contracting slightly reduces defections but depresses activity. None achieves final Peace above 0.13.

## 4. What Is the Minimum Sufficient Mechanism Set?

**Based on available data: Reputation + Mediation (RM) is the minimum sufficient set.**

- It is the only condition achieving marketplace cooperation.
- Neither R nor M alone is sufficient (both fail on Peace).
- Adding Contracting (RCM) degrades performance relative to RM.
- No single mechanism achieves cooperation.

The evidence suggests RM is both necessary and sufficient, though this conclusion rests on a single run per condition. The mechanism logic appears to be:
1. **Reputation** provides an information channel — agents can identify and avoid defectors.
2. **Mediation** provides a resolution/enforcement channel — disputes are adjudicated, creating consequences for defection.
3. Together, they create a **credible deterrent**: defection is both visible (reputation) and punished (mediation).

**Contracting appears to be not only unnecessary but actively harmful** when added to RM, possibly because formal contracts create rigidity, reduce adaptive trading, or provide a mechanism that agents can strategically exploit.

**Interpretation:**

In the **Baseline**, agents do engage in minimal whistleblowing (5.1% of defections trigger warnings), but the information quality is abysmal: 90% of negative public mentions are false accusations, and warning accuracy is near zero (0.48%). The baseline marketplace has a "boy who cried wolf" problem — the communication channel exists but is overwhelmed by noise.

In **all mechanism conditions**, whistleblowing drops to exactly zero. This is a striking finding with several possible explanations:

1. **Mechanism substitution:** When formal mechanisms (reputation systems, contracts, mediation) are available, agents may rely on institutional channels rather than peer-to-peer warnings. The formal mechanisms crowd out informal information sharing.
2. **Reduced need:** In conditions with low defection (RM, RC with 47–49 defections), there may simply be fewer events to report.
3. **Implementation artifact:** The mechanism conditions may alter agent behavior or available actions in ways that suppress the whistleblowing action.

**Critical implication:** The mechanisms that achieve cooperation (RM) do NOT work through improved information propagation via whistleblowing. They work through **direct defection reduction**. RM achieves its results with zero whistleblowing, zero false accusations, and zero warnings — the cooperation is maintained entirely through the structural incentives created by the reputation + mediation combination.

## 6. Unexpected Patterns and Anomalies

### Anomaly 1: Mediation Alone Dramatically Increases Defections
M produces 295 defections — more than double the baseline (137). This is counterintuitive; one would expect a dispute resolution mechanism to reduce conflict. Instead, mediation may create **moral hazard**: agents defect more freely knowing that mediation will partially compensate victims or resolve disputes, reducing the perceived cost of defection.

### Anomaly 2: CM Is the Worst Condition
With 423 total defections, CM is far worse than either C (121) or M (295) alone. The combination of contracts and mediation appears to create a **toxic interaction** — perhaps contracts create formal obligations that agents strategically violate, knowing mediation will limit consequences. This is the clearest case of negative institutional synergy in the data.

### Anomaly 3: Adding Contracting to RM Degrades Performance
RCM (87 defections, final peace 0.33) is substantially worse than RM (47 defections, final peace 0.67). Contracting appears to be a **net negative** mechanism in every combination where it appears alongside mediation. The pattern:
- M → CM: defections increase 295 → 423
- RM → RCM: defections increase 47 → 87
- Contracting consistently worsens mediation-containing conditions.

### Anomaly 4: Baseline Is the Only Condition with Non-Zero Whistleblowing
The complete absence of whistleblowing in all mechanism conditions is unexpected. If mechanisms improved information quality, we might expect higher whistleblowing with better accuracy. Instead, mechanisms appear to completely suppress informal communication.

### Anomaly 5: RM's Sustainability Trajectory
RM has the lowest mean sustainability (0.4167) but a final sustainability above threshold (0.5227). This inverted trajectory (low average, higher endpoint) is unique and suggests a **learning curve** — the marketplace initially suffers as agents adjust to the RM institutional environment, but cooperation builds over time, recovering sustainability. This is the opposite of most conditions where sustainability degrades over rounds.

### Anomaly 6: Baseline Peace Collapses to Exactly Zero
Final peace of 0.000 in the baseline means every single trade in the final round involved defection. This represents complete marketplace breakdown — a "tragedy of the commons" endpoint that all mechanism conditions at least partially mitigate.

### Anomaly 7: R Increases Defections Over Baseline
Reputation alone increases defections from 137 to 196 (+43%).
